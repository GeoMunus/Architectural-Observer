# A Rhapsody in Smoke and Chrome

*A rhapsody in six movements — on war, American culture, the hunger for power,
the waste of what we're given, and the worth of a single human life.*

---

## Movement I — Overture: The Anthem Plays at Halftime
*(quiet, a lone piano, almost a lullaby)*

Somewhere a screen door swings in Ohio,
somewhere a flag folds soft on a porch.
Somewhere a mother is setting the table
with one chair too many, one light like a torch.

We were raised on the hymn of the highway,
on chrome and on neon, on "more, ever more" —
and nobody told us the price of the anthem
is paid by the boy who won't walk through that door.

*Oh say, can you see him?*
*Oh say, did you look?*

---

## Movement II — The Machinery Wakes
*(tempo rising, drums enter like boots on gravel)*

Wake the furnaces! Feed the assembly!
Steel for the eagle and oil for the flame!
Sign here, son — it's glory on paper,
a rifle, a slogan, a number, a name.

They sold us the West and they sold us the moon,
they sold us the ballgame, the bomb, and the tune,
and the jukebox is playing that old marching song —
*sing along, sing along, sing along.*

Milk and honey, drive-thru money,
amber waves turned motor-pool gray —
we can put a man on the moon, my darling,
but we can't seem to bring one home to stay.

---

## Movement III — Chorus of the Hungry Kings
*(operatic, voices stacked, thunder underneath)*

Power! (Power!) — who ordered the power?
A tower of generals toast on the hour!
Caesar in cufflinks, Napoleon's heirs,
trading young futures on old men's dares!

*He wants the mountain! He wants the sea!*
*He wants the pipeline! He wants the lease!*
*Will no one tell him — mercy, mercy —*
*an empire's crown is a dead man's crease?*

Little man signs it, big man denies it,
the market approves and the missiles applaud —
and somewhere a village learns English the hard way:
three syllables, falling — *col-lat-er-al, oh God.*

Bismillah? No! We will not let him go!
(Let him go!) We will not let him go —
he's seventeen, he's seventeen, he's somebody's soul!

---

## Movement IV — The Ledger
*(hard, driving, electric — the angry movement)*

A trillion for thunder, a dime for the schoolhouse,
a fleet for the ocean, a cot for the vet.
We burn through the harvest to gild the arsenal —
how much is enough? They haven't said yet.

Count it! — the copper, the cobalt, the kerosene,
count it! — the acres of crosses in rows.
The ledger runs red and it never runs even:
we spend like we're gods, and we reap what we've sown.

You can't eat a warhead.
You can't drink a drone.
You can't plant a minefield and call it a garden.
You can't hug a headstone and call it a home.

---

## Movement V — Elegy: The Weight of One
*(everything drops away — a single voice, a single string)*

Here is a life: it learned how to whistle.
It burned its first pancakes. It danced badly, once.
It memorized freckles on somebody's shoulder.
It meant to say "sorry." It meant to call home.

There is no medal the size of a heartbeat.
There is no border worth more than a breath.
Whatever the flag, whatever the doctrine —
no cause is holy that harvests a death.

So easy to spend what you didn't create,
so hard to return what you took.
Every life is a library burning —
and God, we had only begun the first book.

---

## Movement VI — Finale: What We Could Be
*(swelling, all voices, then gentling to the end)*

America, I've seen you be gentle —
barn-raisings, blood drives, a stranger's spare tire.
You taught the world jazz; you can unlearn the cannon.
The same hands that launch can pull souls from the fire.

Let the anthem mean supper and everyone seated,
let the rockets be red only Fourth of July,
let "power" mean lifting, let "victory" mean staying,
let no mother rehearse how to say goodbye.

*(softly, the piano returning, as at the beginning)*

Somewhere a screen door swings in Ohio.
Set one less chair out. He's walking up now.
Nothing to win here. Nothing to conquer.
Any way the wind blows —
let it blow him home.

---

*— end —*
